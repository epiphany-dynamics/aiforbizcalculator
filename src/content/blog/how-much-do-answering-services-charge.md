**Answering services typically bill one of three ways: per minute, per call, or a flat monthly tier with an included allowance. The quoted rate covers the base only. Setup, call transfers, after-hours premiums, and overage minutes are billed separately, so the real monthly cost usually lands above the advertised number.**

Most owners shopping for phone coverage compare the headline number on three proposals and pick the smallest one. That comparison is close to meaningless, because the three quotes are almost never measuring the same thing. One counts seconds, one counts calls, one counts a bundle you may not fill. This article stays on one narrow question: what the pricing structures actually are, which fees get left off the quote, and the monthly call volume at which an AI setup becomes the cheaper line item.

## The three ways answering services bill

**Three structures dominate: per minute, per call, and flat monthly with an included allowance. Per minute rewards short calls, per call rewards long ones, and flat tiers reward predictable volume. You cannot compare two quotes until you convert both into a cost per call using your own average call length.**

| Structure | How it is billed | Cheapest when | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per minute | Billed minutes, usually rounded up to the next 30 or 60 seconds | Calls are short, mostly message taking | Rounding turns a 20 second call into a full billed minute |
| Per call | Flat rate per answered call, often with a length cap | Calls run long, such as intake or booking | What happens past the cap, since it often reverts to per minute |
| Flat monthly tier | Fixed fee with an included minute or call allowance | Volume is steady and sits near the tier ceiling | Unused allowance rarely rolls over, and overage prices above base |

### Convert every quote into one number

Use a single comparison metric so structure stops distorting the decision:

**Effective cost per call = total monthly bill / answered calls**

Run each quote at your real volume and your real average call length. A per-minute quote that looks cheap at 90 seconds gets expensive fast at three minutes, and a flat tier that looks expensive at 40 calls looks smart at 300.

## The add-on fees a quote routinely omits

**Base rates are the marketing number. The billable extras are setup, script changes, patch-through transfers, after-hours and holiday premiums, overage minutes, minimum commitments, and calls you never wanted answered. Ask for each one in writing before signing, because each is a real line on the first invoice.**

Walk this checklist through any proposal before you compare it to anything else:

1. Setup or onboarding fee, and whether it is refundable
2. Script writing, and the cost of changing that script later
3. Patch-through or transfer fee, charged per connected call
4. After-hours, weekend, and holiday premiums, and the exact hours that trigger them
5. Overage rate once the included allowance runs out
6. Minimum monthly commitment, whether or not you use it
7. Billing treatment of spam, robocalls, and wrong numbers
8. Bilingual or specialty agent surcharges
9. Integration fees for your CRM or scheduler
10. Contract length, notice period, and cancellation penalty

Items 3, 4, 7, and 10 are the ones that most often surprise people. Spam handling in particular is worth a direct question, since a service that bills every answered call has no incentive to screen aggressively.

## Build your true monthly cost before you compare

**Take the base fee, add billable units times the rate, add transfer fees, add the after-hours premium, add expected overage, then add the setup fee divided across twelve months. Divide the total by answered calls. That per-call number is the only fair basis for comparing a live service against an AI setup.**

**True monthly cost = base + (billable units x rate) + (transfers x transfer fee) + after-hours premium + overage + (setup / 12)**

Illustrative example, using assumed numbers for a plumbing company we will call Riverbend. These figures are hypothetical and chosen for clean math, not drawn from any vendor's published rate card.

| Input (illustrative) | Value |
|---|---|
| Answered calls per month | 320 |
| Average call length | 2.4 minutes, rounded to 3 billed minutes |
| Base fee | $99, includes 100 minutes |
| Overage rate | $1.45 per minute |
| Transfer fee | $0.75, on 40 percent of calls |
| After-hours premium | 15 percent, on 25 percent of calls |
| Setup fee | $150, amortized over 12 months |

Billed minutes come to 960. Subtract the 100 included and 860 overage minutes cost $1,247. Transfers add $96. The after-hours premium adds roughly $52. Setup amortizes to $12.50. Total: about $1,507 per month, or **$4.71 per answered call** against a $99 headline.

The gap between $99 and $1,507 is the entire point. The base fee is a deposit on a variable bill.

**Break-even calls per month = (AI fixed cost - live service fixed cost) / (live variable cost per call - AI variable cost per call)**

Continuing the illustrative Riverbend numbers:

- Live service fixed cost: $99 base plus $12.50 amortized setup = $111.50
- Live service variable cost per call: about $4.81 once overage minutes, expected transfers, and the after-hours premium are blended in
- AI fixed cost (illustrative): $299 platform subscription plus $100 amortized implementation = $399
- AI variable cost per call (illustrative): about $0.45 in usage and telephony at 2.4 minutes

Break-even = ($399 - $111.50) / ($4.81 - $0.45) = $287.50 / $4.36 = **about 66 calls per month**

Three things move that number hard. Longer average calls push break-even down, because per-minute billing punishes length. A cheaper AI implementation pushes it down too: at a $199 subscription with a $600 build amortized over a year, break-even lands near 30 calls. And a live service with a generous included allowance pushes it up, because more of its cost is fixed.

## When break-even is the wrong test

**Break-even only compares cost. It says nothing about whether either option answers the call well. If your calls involve nuanced triage, distressed customers, or high-value bookings, a slightly more expensive option that converts better wins on revenue even when it loses on price.**

Two situations where you should ignore the crossover point. First, very low volume: under roughly 40 or 50 calls a month, both options are cheap in absolute dollars and the decision should turn on quality and coverage hours, not a $30 difference. Second, complex call types: if a mishandled call costs you a five-figure job, the correct comparison is revenue captured per call, not cost per call.

Pricing structure is also not the same as value. Answering the call at all is usually worth more than either fee, which is the argument laid out in [the value of fewer missed calls](/blog/ai-receptionist-roi/). ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is per-minute or per-call billing cheaper?

It depends entirely on your average call length. Per-minute billing favors short message-taking calls, especially at 30 second rounding increments. Per-call billing favors longer calls such as appointment booking or intake, provided the length cap is generous. Calculate both at your actual average length before deciding.

### Why is my answering service bill higher than the quote?

Almost always overage plus per-event fees. The base tier includes a fixed allowance, and once you exceed it every minute prices above the effective base rate. Transfer fees, after-hours premiums, and billed spam calls stack on top. Ask for a sample invoice at your expected volume, not a rate sheet.

### Do answering services charge for spam or wrong-number calls?

Many do, since the call was answered. Policies vary and some credit obvious robocalls on request. Get the policy in writing, along with whether credits are automatic or require you to dispute each call, which is administrative work you will not do consistently.

### At what call volume does AI answering get cheaper?

Solve the break-even formula with your own numbers rather than using a benchmark. In the illustrative example above, the crossover sits near 66 calls per month, but it moves substantially with implementation cost, subscription tier, and average call length. Longer calls lower the break-even point.

### Should setup fees factor into monthly comparisons?

Yes, amortized. Divide the one-time fee across the term you realistically expect to stay, commonly twelve months, and add that to the monthly cost of both options. Ignoring setup makes a high-build, low-subscription option look artificially cheap in month one.
