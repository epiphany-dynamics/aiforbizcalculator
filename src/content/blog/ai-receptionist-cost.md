**AI receptionist pricing comes in three shapes: per connected minute, per answered call, and a flat monthly base with included minutes. Which one is cheapest depends on two numbers you already have: monthly call volume and average call length. Setup, integration, and overage fees usually decide the real total.**

Every quote you collect will look like a single number. It is not. Underneath it sits a rate structure that behaves very differently at 80 calls a month than at 600, and a stack of one-time and pass-through charges that rarely appear on the pricing page. This article stays strictly on the cost side. What fewer missed calls are worth is a separate calculation, covered in [AI Receptionist ROI: The Value of Fewer Missed Calls](/blog/ai-receptionist-roi/).

All figures below are clearly labeled illustrative planning assumptions. Use them to build the model, then drop your own quoted rates into the same formulas.

## The three pricing models and what each one charges

**Vendors bill on connected minutes, on answered calls, or on a flat monthly base with a minute allowance and an overage rate. A fourth hybrid charges per completed outcome such as a booked appointment. Each model has a different sensitivity: minutes punish long calls, per-call punishes high volume, flat punishes underuse.**

| Model | Monthly cost formula | Cheapest when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per connected minute | Calls × avg minutes × rate | Calls are short and volume is unpredictable | Long or repetitive callers inflate the bill |
| Per answered call | Calls × per-call rate | Calls run long but volume is modest | Spam and hangups may still count as answered |
| Flat monthly + allowance | Base + (minutes over allowance × overage) | Volume is steady and above the break-even | Overage rate above your usage rate erases the savings |
| Per outcome (booking, qualified lead) | Outcomes × rate | Booking rate is low relative to call volume | Definition of a qualifying outcome is the vendor's |

Two definitions decide whether a quote is comparable. First, when the meter starts: at ring, at answer, or at first spoken word. Second, whether transfer time is billed. If the AI answers, qualifies, then warm transfers to your team, some vendors bill the entire session including the handoff, which can add a minute or more to every transferred call.

## The fees a quote leaves out

**The recurring rate is usually the smaller half of year one. Setup, integration build, telephony pass-through, and per-message SMS charges are billed separately and often quoted only after you ask. Amortize every one-time fee across twelve months before you compare two vendors.**

The formula that makes quotes comparable:

**True first-year monthly cost = recurring monthly + (one-time fees ÷ 12) + pass-through usage**

Ask for a written yes or no on each of these before signing:

1. Setup or onboarding fee, and whether it is refundable if you cancel in month one
2. Integration build cost per system: calendar, CRM, field service software, EHR or practice management
3. Number porting fee and monthly telephony charges, if separate from the platform rate
4. Whether warm transfer minutes are billed, and to which side of the call
5. Whether spam calls, wrong numbers, and sub-ten-second hangups are billable
6. SMS or email follow-up pricing, per message or bundled
7. Script and prompt revision fees after the initial build
8. After-hours versus 24/7 tiering, and whether weekends carry a different rate
9. Minimum contract term, and the renewal price escalator
10. Per-location or per-extension multipliers if you run more than one site

That checklist is the receptionist-specific version of a broader pattern. ## The call volume where each model becomes cheapest

**Two crossovers matter. Per-minute beats per-call whenever your average call runs shorter than the per-call rate divided by the per-minute rate. Flat monthly beats usage billing once volume passes the base fee divided by the cost of a single average call. Both are arithmetic, not judgment.**

### Crossover one: minutes versus per call

Break-even call length = per-call rate ÷ per-minute rate

Illustrative assumption: $1.75 per answered call versus $0.45 per connected minute. Break-even length is 1.75 ÷ 0.45, which is 3.9 minutes. If your calls average 3.2 minutes, per-minute billing costs $1.44 per call and wins. If your intake runs six minutes because you collect insurance details or job scope, per-call billing wins by a wide margin.

### Crossover two: usage versus flat monthly

Break-even call volume = monthly base ÷ (avg minutes × per-minute rate)

Illustrative assumption: a $299 base against $0.45 per minute at 3.2 minutes per call. Break-even is 299 ÷ 1.44, which is about 208 calls a month. Below that, usage billing is cheaper. Above it, the flat plan is.

The trap is the overage rate. If the plan includes 1,000 minutes and charges $0.40 per minute beyond, the flat plan stays cheaper at every volume above the crossover, because the overage rate sits below the pure usage rate. Flip the overage to $0.55 and the plan has a ceiling: past the allowance, roughly 312 calls at 3.2 minutes each, you are paying more per minute than the usage vendor charges. Always compare the overage rate to the usage rate, not to the base fee.

Trade businesses with heavy seasonal swings should run the crossover at both their slow month and their peak month, since a plan sized for July can be badly wrong in February. ## A worked first-year cost at 300 calls a month

**At 300 calls averaging 3.2 minutes, the illustrative flat plan wins on headline rate. Once setup, integration, and telephony are amortized, the real first-year monthly cost lands about 60 percent above the advertised base. That gap, not the rate card, is what usually decides between two vendors.**

Illustrative assumptions: 300 calls per month, 3.2 minutes average, 960 total minutes.

| Line item | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Flat base, 1,000 minutes included | $299.00 |
| Setup fee $750, amortized over 12 months | $62.50 |
| Calendar and CRM integration $1,200, amortized | $100.00 |
| Telephony pass-through at $0.02 per minute | $19.20 |
| **True first-year monthly cost** | **$480.70** |

The same 300 calls under the usage model cost $432 in rate alone, and $613 once the same one-time fees are amortized. Under per-call billing they cost $525, or $706 loaded. Ranked on headline rate the flat plan wins by $133. Ranked on first-year loaded cost it wins by $132, which is close enough that contract term and overage terms should break the tie.

## When an AI receptionist is not the cheaper option

**Below roughly 60 calls a month, almost any flat plan costs more per call than the alternative, and amortized setup dominates the total. Calls requiring clinical, legal, or pricing judgment on the first contact are also poor fits, because every escalation still consumes a billed minute and a staff minute.**

Two more exceptions worth naming. If your calls are dominated by existing customers asking one specific question that a well-written text autoresponder answers, you are buying voice infrastructure for a text problem. And if your systems have no API, the integration line becomes a custom build, which moves the one-time cost from hundreds to thousands and pushes the twelve-month amortization above the recurring fee.

Cost is only half the decision. Once you have a defensible monthly number, put it against the value of the calls you are currently losing, then decide. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does an AI receptionist cost per month?

It depends on the model and your volume. Using the illustrative rates in this article, 300 calls a month at 3.2 minutes each lands between $299 and $525 in recurring charges, and between $481 and $706 once setup, integration, and telephony are amortized across the first year. Always request the loaded first-year number in writing.

### Is per-minute or per-call pricing cheaper?

Divide the per-call rate by the per-minute rate. That gives a break-even call length. Shorter average calls favor per-minute billing, longer ones favor per-call. At an illustrative $1.75 per call and $0.45 per minute, the switch happens at 3.9 minutes.

### Do AI receptionist vendors charge setup fees?

Many do, typically framed as onboarding, script build, or integration work. These are one-time but real. Divide them by twelve and add the result to the monthly rate, otherwise a vendor with a low base and a high setup fee will look cheaper than it is.

### What call volume makes a flat monthly plan worth it?

Divide the monthly base by the cost of one average call under usage billing. At a $299 base and $1.44 per average call, the crossover is about 208 calls a month. Then check that the plan's overage rate is below the usage rate, or the savings stop at the allowance.

### Should I count spam calls in my volume estimate?

Yes, unless the contract explicitly excludes them from billing. Spam and wrong numbers can be a meaningful share of inbound volume, and under per-call billing each one is chargeable. Ask how the vendor defines a billable call and whether sub-ten-second sessions are excluded.
