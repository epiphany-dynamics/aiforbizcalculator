**A dedicated human virtual receptionist is usually priced per minute or per call on top of a monthly base, with quotes structured as a bundled block of time plus an overage rate. Your real cost is base plus overage plus transfer and after-hours fees, divided by calls actually handled.**

## What you are actually buying when you buy a virtual receptionist

**Four different products share the name. A dedicated virtual receptionist is one assigned person on your phones. A virtual assistant does admin work with calls as a side duty. An answering service is a shared pool. An AI receptionist is software. Each bills on a different unit, so quotes do not compare directly.**

| Model | Billing unit | Common structure | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated virtual receptionist | Minute or call, over a base | Monthly base with bundled minutes, then overage | You want one named person who learns your callers |
| General virtual assistant | Hour | Hourly retainer or block of hours | Calls are a small slice of a wider admin load |
| Answering service pool | Minute or call | Tiered bundles, shared agents | Coverage matters more than familiarity |
| AI receptionist | Minute, call, or seat | Subscription plus usage | Volume is high and intake is repetitive |

The distinction is not branding. A dedicated receptionist charges you for a person's availability, so you pay whether the phone rings or not. A pool charges for consumption. That is why an hourly comparison against a virtual assistant misleads: our breakdown of [virtual assistant cost vs automation](/blog/virtual-assistant-cost/) prices general admin labor, not phone coverage, and the two rate cards do not line up. Software is a third shape again, priced closer to usage than to headcount, which is the point of [AI receptionist cost: what the three pricing models charge](/blog/ai-receptionist-cost/).

## The five variables that set the invoice

**Five inputs drive almost every dedicated receptionist quote: billed minutes, call volume, coverage hours, transfer or patch handling, and the overage rate past your bundle. Change any one and the monthly number moves. Get all five in writing before you compare two vendors, because most quotes only publish the first.**

### Billed minutes and how they are counted

Billed minutes are not talk minutes. Most vendors round up to a 30 or 60 second increment, apply a one minute minimum, and add post call wrap time for logging and message delivery. A 2.4 minute conversation can bill at 3.0 minutes. Across 600 calls that rounding alone is 360 minutes you did not talk for.

### Coverage hours and the after-hours line

Business hours coverage is the base price. Evenings, weekends, and holidays usually carry a premium, either a flat monthly add-on or a higher per minute rate. Ask whether after-hours minutes draw from the same bundle or bill separately. Those two answers produce very different invoices at the same call volume.

### Transfers, overages, and the ceiling you did not price

Warm transfers, appointment booking into your calendar, and outbound callbacks are frequently priced per action on top of minutes. The overage rate matters more than the base rate once you exceed the bundle, and almost every growing business exceeds the bundle.

## The formula: monthly cost and cost per handled call

**Monthly cost equals base plan plus overage minutes times the overage rate plus per transfer fees plus after hours premium. Divide that by calls handled to get cost per handled call, the only number that compares a dedicated receptionist against a pool service or software honestly.**

```
Billed minutes    = Calls x Average billed minutes per call (after rounding + wrap)
Overage minutes   = max(0, Billed minutes - Included minutes)
Monthly cost      = Base plan
                  + (Overage minutes x Overage rate)
                  + (Transfers x Transfer fee)
                  + After-hours premium
Cost per handled call = Monthly cost / Calls handled
```

Use calls handled, not calls received. If the vendor abandons or voicemails a share of your traffic, that share still cost you the base rate and produced nothing. The same discipline applies to per resolved contact pricing in [customer service outsourcing pricing: cost per resolved contact](/blog/customer-service-outsourcing-pricing/).

## An illustrative example: 600 calls a month

**Assume 600 calls a month, three billed minutes per call after rounding and wrap time, 180 warm transfers, and evening coverage. Those illustrative inputs produce 1,800 billed minutes. The same volume priced against three bundle sizes swings the invoice by roughly 750 dollars a month and the per call cost by over a dollar.**

All figures below are illustrative, chosen to show how the formula behaves. Transfers are 180 at $0.75 ($135) and the evening premium is a flat $250 in every scenario.

| Plan | Base | Included minutes | Overage rate | Overage cost | Total monthly | Cost per handled call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: small bundle | $1,200 | 1,000 | $1.25 | $1,000 | $2,585 | $4.31 |
| B: right sized | $1,850 | 1,800 | $1.10 | $0 | $2,235 | $3.73 |
| C: oversized | $2,600 | 3,000 | $0.95 | $0 | $2,985 | $4.98 |

Two lessons fall out. Plan A looks cheapest on the rate card and costs the most per call because 800 minutes bill at the penalty rate. Plan C has the lowest overage rate and the worst result, because you prepaid for 1,200 minutes you never used. The bundle that matches your actual billed minutes wins, and you cannot pick it until you have measured average billed minutes per call.

## Where the quote hides money

**The base rate is rarely where the money leaks. Setup and script build, billing increments, holiday premiums, minimum contract terms, message delivery fees, and post call wrap time all sit below the headline number. Ask for each in writing and rerun your cost per handled call with them included.**

Work this checklist with any vendor before comparing:

1. What is the billing increment, and is there a per call minimum?
2. Is wrap time billed, and how much does it typically add per call?
3. Do after-hours minutes draw from the bundle or bill separately?
4. What is the overage rate, and does it step down at any threshold?
5. Are transfers, bookings, and outbound callbacks priced per action?
6. Is there a setup or script build fee, and is it refundable?
7. What is the contract term, and can the bundle be resized mid term?

Pool services publish some of these differently again, which is why the tiering in [how much do answering services charge](/blog/how-much-do-answering-services-charge/) is worth reading alongside a dedicated quote.

## When a dedicated virtual receptionist is the wrong buy

**Skip the dedicated model when volume is under roughly 100 calls a month, when calls cluster in short unpredictable bursts, or when most calls are one line questions about hours and location. In those cases you pay a base rate for idle capacity, and a pool service or software handles it for less.**

The dedicated model earns its premium when callers are high value, when the conversation needs judgment, or when a familiar voice materially affects whether someone books. If your average call is a two sentence question and your average customer is worth $60, the math rarely clears. Price the alternative honestly before you assume the human is worth it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is virtual receptionist pricing different from answering service pricing?

A dedicated virtual receptionist prices a person's availability, so the base covers reserved capacity for your account. An answering service prices consumption from a shared pool. At the same volume, the dedicated option usually carries a higher base and a lower marginal cost per call.

### What is a reasonable overage rate to accept?

Judge it relative to your blended rate, not in isolation. If your base plan works out to $1.03 per included minute and the overage rate is $1.25, you are paying about a 21 percent penalty. Anything approaching double your blended rate signals that the bundle is deliberately undersized.

### Should I pay per call or per minute?

Per call favors you when conversations run long, since a 6 minute booking bills the same as a 90 second question. Per minute favors you when most calls are short. Calculate both against your actual call length distribution rather than your average, because averages hide the long tail that drives the bill.

### Does a dedicated receptionist cost more than an AI receptionist?

Almost always, on a per call basis. The comparison that matters is cost per handled call against booking rate. If the human books meaningfully more of the same calls, the higher cost can still win. If booking rates are close, the price gap decides it.

### What happens to my bill if volume doubles?

Everything past the bundle bills at the overage rate, so doubling volume can more than double the invoice. Ask for the resize terms before signing: whether you can move up a tier mid contract, and whether unused minutes on the larger plan roll over or expire monthly.
